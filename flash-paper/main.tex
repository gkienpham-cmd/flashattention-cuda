\title{Roofline-Guided Characterization (RGD) of Attention Decode on Blackwell:\\
From Per-CTA-Bound to Work-Starvation Across MHA, GQA, and MLA}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Kien Pham}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Tufts University}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Attention decode is the latency-critical phase of large language model (LLM)
serving, yet no published work provides a \emph{prediction-vs-measured} roofline
characterization on NVIDIA's latest datacenter GPU (B300 / \texttt{sm\_103})
across attention types and precisions. We present \emph{Roofline-Guided Decode}
(RGD): twelve attention kernels of increasing sophistication (naive, tiled,
fused, split-KV, GQA-packed, score-stationary, FP8, NVFP4, MLA, and tensor-core
\texttt{tcgen05}), each paired with a pre-registered two-layer roofline
prediction (pure roofline plus a per-CTA-corrected layer) and evaluated against
measured results on Tesla T4, A100, B200, and B300. Our central finding is that decode's apparent per-CTA
throughput ceiling is \emph{work-starvation}: the right engine
(\texttt{tcgen05} tensor cores) converts work into throughput
($0.75 \to 1785\tflops$ on B300), but only in the high-throughput serving regime
(large batch $\times$ long context). At single-stream, moderate-context decode,
even NVIDIA's production kernel runs at 1--2\% of the FP8 peak. Across the seven
decode steps the per-CTA-corrected layer matched the measured limiter every time,
while pure roofline predicted the wrong regime at every single-stream step. As a
pre-registered boundary test, we confirm on B300 that native NVFP4 \emph{compute}
does not accelerate MLA decode: both FP4 and FP8 GEMMs remain HBM-bound at the
$M{=}128$ decode shape, reaching at most 5.8\% and 21.7\% of their tensor-core
peaks. We
\emph{complement}, not beat, production kernels: the contribution is the open,
prediction-vs-measured characterization, the two-layer model, and the boundary
results.
\end{abstract}
